How the scam works: callers pretend to be police (for example Mumbai or Delhi Police), CBI, the Narcotics department, RBI, the telecom department or the Enforcement Directorate, often from spoofed numbers or automated IVR calls.

They accuse the victim of crimes such as a courier or FedEx/DHL parcel containing drugs, fake passports or SIM cards, misuse of their Aadhaar or mobile number, money laundering or terror funding, or they claim a family member has been caught or had an accident.

In a "digital arrest" the victim is told to stay on a Skype or WhatsApp video call, sometimes in front of a fake police-station set, until they pay. The criminals then demand money to "settle" the fake case. I4C says these are organised, often cross-border, crime syndicates.

Key facts and precautions: there is no concept of digital arrest in Indian law, and real law enforcement does not arrest anyone over a video call. Do not share personal or financial details over phone, email or chat without verifying who is asking. Verify the caller by contacting your nearest police station yourself. Talk to family or friends before transferring any money. Report immediately at www.cybercrime.gov.in or by calling 1930.
